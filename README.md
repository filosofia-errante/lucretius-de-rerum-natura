## why epicurus?

epicurus and epicurean philosophy have often been misunderstood or reduced to simple hedonism. in reality, epicureanism offers a profound, empirical, and liberating worldview grounded in peace of mind, scientific inquiry, and freedom from irrational fears. 

throughout history, some of the most influential and independent-minded thinkers across centuries have drawn deep inspiration from epicurus:

* **lucretius**: in *de rerum natura*, lucretius immortalized epicurus as the brave greek thinker who first dared to raise his eyes against the oppressive terror of religion, breaking through nature's gates to bring back truth and liberate human minds: *"a greek it was who first opposing dared raise mortal eyes that terror to withstand... and thus his will and hardy wisdom won; and forward thus he fared afar, beyond the flaming ramparts of the world..."*
* **thomas jefferson**: the third u.s. president and primary author of the declaration of independence proudly declared his philosophical allegiance in an 1819 letter: *"i too am an epicurean. i consider the genuine (as distinguished from the imputed) doctrines of epicurus as containing everything rational in moral philosophy which greece and rome have left us."*
* **karl marx**: in 1841, marx chose epicurean physics as the subject of his doctoral thesis (*the difference between the democritean and epicurean philosophy of nature*), admiring epicurus for introducing the atomic "swerve" which created room for human agency and freedom.
* **friedrich nietzsche**: the german philosopher frequently praised epicurus for his mental independence and tranquil wisdom, writing in *the wanderer and his shadow* that *"epicurus has lived in all ages and lives still, unknown to those who called themselves epicureans..."*
* **seneca the younger**: though a stoic philosopher, seneca frequently quoted epicurus with admiration throughout his *letters from a stoic*, noting: *"i am wont to cross over even into the enemy's camp,—not as a deserter, but as a scout,"* recognizing the timeless brilliance of epicurean insight.
* **lucian of samosata**: the ancient satirist praised epicurus in his work *alexander the false prophet*, celebrating how epicurus's writings brought truth, clear reasoning, and liberation from superstition: *"what blessings that book creates for its readers! what peace, tranquility, and freedom it produces in them, releasing them from fears, illusions, and marvels..."*

by digitizing lucretius's *de rerum natura* ("on the nature of things"), this project aims to preserve and present epicurean thought in its clearest, most accessible web-native format for modern readers.
